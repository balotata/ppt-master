# Narrative System

Use this file to build the story, slide claims, outline, page types, copy, and speaker notes. For web keynote decks, the first job is not to summarize the topic. The first job is to find the conflict that makes the topic worth presenting.

## Core Tension

Before designing substantial decks, define:

- Topic: one plain sentence.
- Core tension: the real conflict underneath the topic, e.g. freedom vs loop, speed vs trust, automation vs agency, growth vs debt, beauty vs control, convenience vs consequence.
- Thesis: one sentence with a point of view.
- Audience: who must believe, feel, decide, or do something.
- Arc: how the story moves.
- Omission rule: what can be left out without weakening the point.

Prefer arcs with pressure:

- `pain -> mechanism -> consequence -> principle`
- `myth -> reality -> system -> strategy`
- `past -> friction -> breakthrough -> future`
- `case -> pattern -> model -> action`
- `surface -> mechanism -> tradeoff -> decision`

## Slide Contract

Every non-appendix slide should answer one question:

- What should the audience believe after this slide?
- What makes that belief credible or emotionally clear?
- What visual structure makes it readable?
- What can be omitted?

For analytical, financial, scientific, legal, policy, or investor decks, use explicit proof objects: charts, tables, timelines, matrices, source excerpts, diagrams, or product evidence.

For visual keynote decks, proof can be broader: a dominant image, contrast scene, quote, typographic poster, annotated example, mechanism drawing, or carefully staged comparison may carry the slide if it makes the claim legible. Do not force a chart onto a slide that wants to be a cinematic beat.

If two ideas compete, split them. If a slide cannot support a point, demote it to appendix or delete it.

## Page Types

Use these as building blocks, not rigid templates:

- Cover / thesis
- Core tension
- Agenda as argument
- Market or context frame
- Case frame
- Timeline
- Mechanism
- Loop diagram
- Data highlight
- Chart proof
- Quote / punchline
- Comparison
- Matrix
- Product or system map
- Process
- Risk boundary
- Decision page
- Principles
- Roadmap
- Source / appendix
- Closing thesis

## Page Allocation

For a typical 10-16 page keynote:

- 1 page cover thesis.
- 1-2 pages context, case, or tension.
- 3-6 pages mechanism or argument development.
- 2-4 pages evidence, comparison, risk, or counterargument.
- 1-2 pages synthesis, principle, roadmap, or decision.
- 1 page closing or source page.

Do not follow document order by default. Extract the strongest conflict, evidence, consequence, and action.

## Claim Title Craft

Every slide needs a point-making title or thesis line with charm. It should be sharp, concise, elegant, and specific enough that the audience remembers it after the slide changes.

Good titles:

- Say the conclusion, not the category.
- Carry tension: before/after, myth/reality, cost/benefit, surface/mechanism, promise/debt.
- Use concrete nouns and active verbs.
- Stay short enough to read at a glance.
- Feel spoken, not slogan-stuffed.

Weak: `Revenue and margin trends`  
Stronger: `Growth slowed, but the margin engine kept expanding.`

Weak: `AI agent architecture`  
Stronger: `The agent layer moves work from screens to delegated loops.`

Weak: `AI 与多模态表型`  
Stronger: `睡眠图谱，开始有了通用底座。`

Weak: `动态睡眠暴露`  
Stronger: `平均时长骗过了我们，轨迹不会。`

Weak: `后续关注`  
Stronger: `真正要等的，不是通报，是责任链。`

Before building, rewrite slide titles until they pass the read-alone test: if the audience saw only the title and visual, they would still understand the argument.

## Copy Rules

- Write for a speaker's mouth, not a whitepaper.
- Use short, exact sentences.
- Prefer short sentence bursts over academic long sentences.
- Delete filler: "由此可见", "总而言之", "在某种程度上", "赋能", "生态闭环" unless the deck defines them concretely.
- Avoid generic AI phrasing and consulting wallpaper.
- Bind abstract claims to systems, behaviors, tradeoffs, data, or examples.
- Default Chinese should feel sharp, spoken, and human. Prefer a sentence with judgment over a neutral topic summary.
- Use rhetorical pressure when useful: contrast, reversal, punchline, concrete consequence.
- Bilingual decks should be meaning-matched, not word-for-word translated.
- Let philosophical tone come from rhythm and precision, not terminology.

## Outline Checkpoint

For heavy, long, research-backed, or image-heavy decks, provide or internally lock a Markdown outline before building unless the user asked for immediate production. Include per slide:

- Page type.
- Claim title.
- Core content or proof.
- Visual direction.
- Image/visual need: none / hero / per-slide custom image / real-source image / proof-object / typographic poster.
- Speaker note intent.
- Source requirement or uncertainty.

For fast creative requests, proceed directly but still keep the same mental structure.

## Speaker Notes

Use speaker notes when the deck is for live delivery, executive persuasion, teaching, or complex analysis.

Good notes:

- Explain the conflict in plain language.
- Add one example, analogy, or caveat not already on the slide.
- Keep a spoken rhythm.
- Do not simply read the slide.

## Existing Material Transformation

When turning proposals, papers, reports, transcripts, or notes into slides:

- Preserve exact terms, numbers, definitions, and citations when they matter.
- Rewrite the order around argument strength, not source order.
- Convert dense paragraphs into visual structures: framework, comparison, timeline, mechanism map, matrix, annotated quote, or data highlight.
- Keep the author's best idea, but make the slide feel newly authored.
- If the original is visually weak, rebuild around the actual claim instead of beautifying the same layout.
